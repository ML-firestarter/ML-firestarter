# Unit economics

## What is CAC?

- [ ] The cost of serving one customer for a month
- [ ] The gross profit a customer brings in over their lifetime
- [x] Sales and marketing spending divided by the number of new customers it brought in
- [ ] The monthly price a customer pays

CAC is the customer acquisition cost. The gross profit over a customer's lifetime is the LTV.

## A customer pays \$100 a month at a 75% gross margin, and monthly churn is 2.5%. What's the LTV?

- [ ] \$75
- [ ] \$900
- [x] \$3,000
- [ ] \$4,000

\$100 × 75% = \$75 of gross profit a month, and the average customer stays 1 ÷ 2.5% = 40 months: 40 × \$75 = \$3,000. \$4,000 would count revenue instead of gross profit.

## Winning a customer costs \$900, and each customer brings in \$75 of gross profit a month. What's the payback period?

- [ ] 9 months
- [x] 12 months
- [ ] 40 months
- [ ] 3.3 months

\$900 ÷ \$75 = 12 months of gross profit to earn back the CAC.

## What does the common rule of thumb say?

- [x] LTV should be at least three times CAC, and payback should take no more than about a year
- [ ] CAC should be at least three times LTV
- [ ] LTV and CAC should be about equal
- [ ] Payback should take at least three years

A customer should bring in well over what it cost to win them, and fast enough to pay back that cost.

## A business loses money on each customer. What happens as it grows?

- [ ] Growth fixes it, because costs are spread over more customers
- [x] The losses grow, because each new customer costs more to win and serve than they bring in
- [ ] Nothing changes, because churn stays the same
- [ ] It depends only on the ARR

If each customer brings in less than it costs to win and serve them, growth only makes the losses bigger.
