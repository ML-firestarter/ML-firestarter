# Gross margin

## An AI writing app earns \$1 million in a quarter. It pays \$350,000 for model API calls and \$50,000 for hosting and support. What's its gross margin?

- [ ] 40%
- [x] 60%
- [ ] 65%
- [ ] 95%

The gross profit is \$1,000,000 − \$400,000 = \$600,000, and \$600,000 ÷ \$1,000,000 = 60%. Hosting and support count too, not only the API calls.

## Which of these are part of an AI product's cost of goods sold?

- [x] GPU time for inference
- [ ] Salaries of the research team
- [x] Hosting
- [x] Customer support
- [ ] Marketing

COGS is what it costs to deliver the product itself. Research, sales and marketing are paid out of the gross profit that's left.

## Why do AI products usually have lower gross margins than traditional software?

- [ ] AI companies spend more on research
- [x] Every response takes computation, so each user costs real money to serve
- [ ] AI products are always sold at lower prices
- [ ] AI products need bigger sales teams

Serving one more user of traditional software costs almost nothing, so margins of 70–90% are common. Research and sales costs don't count toward COGS, so they don't lower the gross margin.

## In unit economics, what counts from each customer?

- [ ] The revenue they bring in
- [x] The gross profit they bring in
- [ ] Their revenue minus all the company's costs
- [ ] The number of months they stay, whatever they pay

A customer paying \$100 a month at a 75% gross margin brings in \$75 a month. That's the number LTV is built from.

## Which of these raise an AI product's gross margin?

- [x] Cheaper GPU time for each response
- [ ] Hiring more researchers
- [x] A higher price for the same usage
- [ ] Spending less on marketing

Gross margin depends only on revenue and the cost of delivering the product. Research and marketing are paid out of the gross profit, so they don't change it.
