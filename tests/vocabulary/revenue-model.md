# Revenue model

## What three questions does a revenue model answer?

- [ ] How many customers there are, how long they stay and what they cost to win
- [x] Who pays, what they pay for, and how the price is set
- [ ] What it costs to build, host and sell the product
- [ ] What the revenue was last year, is this year and will be next year

The same product can be sold in different ways, and the revenue model is the choice between them.

## How do AI APIs usually charge?

- [ ] A flat monthly fee with no limits
- [ ] Per request, however long it is
- [x] Per million tokens, with output tokens costing more than input tokens
- [ ] Per million tokens, with input and output tokens at the same price

It's usage-based pricing: you pay for what you use.

## What's a free tier with limits that nudge heavy users to pay called?

- [ ] Per-seat pricing
- [x] Freemium
- [ ] Advertising
- [ ] An enterprise contract

The free tier brings users in, and its limits nudge the heaviest ones toward a paid plan.

## A chat app charges \$20 a month, and each message costs it about \$0.004 of GPU time. How much does a user who sends 1,000 messages a day cost it?

- [ ] About \$4 a month
- [ ] About \$3.60 a month
- [ ] About \$12 a month
- [x] About \$120 a month, six times what they pay

1,000 messages a day × 30 days × \$0.004 = \$120 a month. A user who sends 30 messages a day costs about \$3.60 a month, which leaves a gross margin of 82%.

## Why do flat AI subscriptions usually come with usage limits?

- [x] Every response takes GPU time, so a heavy user can cost more than they pay
- [ ] Models can only answer a fixed number of questions
- [ ] Limits make the answers more accurate
- [ ] Limits are required for per-seat pricing

Serving one more user of traditional software costs almost nothing, but every AI response takes GPU time to generate. That's also why AI pricing often follows usage.
