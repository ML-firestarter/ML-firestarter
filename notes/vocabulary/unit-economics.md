# Unit economics

Unit economics looks at a business one customer (or one sale) at a time: does each one bring in more than it costs to win and to serve? If not, growth only makes the losses bigger.

The usual numbers for a subscription business:

- **CAC** (customer acquisition cost): sales and marketing spending divided by the number of new customers it brought in.
- **LTV** (lifetime value): the gross profit a customer brings in over their whole time as a customer. A simple estimate is monthly revenue per customer × [gross margin](gross-margin.md) ÷ monthly [churn](churn.md).
- **Payback period**: how many months of gross profit it takes to earn back the CAC.

A common rule of thumb is that LTV should be at least three times CAC, and that payback should take no more than about a year. For AI products the cost of serving each customer matters more than usual, because every request takes computation: a heavy user on a flat monthly price can bring in less than they cost.

**Example:** a customer pays \$100 a month at a 75% gross margin, which is \$75 of gross profit a month. Monthly churn is 2.5%, so the average customer stays 40 months and the LTV is 40 × \$75 = \$3,000. Winning a customer costs \$900. LTV/CAC is 3.3, and the payback period is \$900 ÷ \$75 = 12 months.
