# ARR

ARR says how much revenue a subscription business would bring in over the next twelve months if nothing changed: no new customers, no cancellations and no price changes. Its monthly counterpart is MRR (monthly recurring revenue), and ARR = 12 × MRR.

Only recurring revenue counts. One-time fees for setup, consulting or hardware are left out, because they won't come back next year. AI companies with usage-based pricing often quote an *annualized revenue run rate* instead: the latest month's revenue × 12. It can move quickly in both directions, because usage isn't fixed by a contract.

ARR is a snapshot, not a forecast and not an accounting figure: it isn't revenue already earned, and it doesn't subtract any costs. It's watched closely because it shows growth from month to month, and investors often value subscription companies as a multiple of it. It grows with new customers and expansion (existing customers paying more), and shrinks with [churn](churn.md) (customers leaving or paying less).

**Example:** a company has 200 customers paying \$500 a month each. Its MRR is \$100,000 and its ARR is \$1.2 million. If it also earned \$50,000 from one-time setup fees this year, that doesn't count toward ARR.
