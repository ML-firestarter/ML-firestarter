# Workbook

The lessons teach one idea at a time, but a real model needs several at once. This workbook has ten exercises to practise on, and each of them mixes ideas from the lessons in a way the lessons didn't: standardizing with training, a device with a dictionary of tensors, saving with searching.

They go from the easiest, which takes a few minutes, to the hardest, which can take an evening. Each one says which lessons it draws on, so you know where to look when you get stuck. The first ones work on whole tensors, and the middle ones train models and measure them. The last ones build modules, stop a training early and keep the best model.

You work on them as you do on the exercises at the end of the lessons. You write the code in the page, **Check** tells you which parts of it work, and once they all do, you can compare your code with our solution. The numbers in the tables are the ones that PyTorch gives on a computer too, give or take the last digit of a decimal.

The workbook is for practice, so it doesn't count toward a certificate. Take the exercises in order the first time through, and come back to the ones you want to practise more.

> [!TIP]
> When an exercise looks like too much, don't write all of it at once. Make the first check pass, then the next one. Each check shows the call it makes and what it expects, so you always know what to work on next. Printing a tensor's shape, with `print(x.shape)`, solves most of the mysteries.
